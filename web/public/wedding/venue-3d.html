Night lighting checklist
Faces first. Front-light the stage from the two wash towers at ~45°, 2700–3200 K, CRI ≥ 90 — decorative bulbs alone leave faces dark in photos and video.
Flicker-free fixtures for anything on camera; cheap LED strings and PARs flicker on video.
Keep it warm (2200–2700 K) for Edison bulbs and arches; use colour only on the backdrop, never on faces.
On dimmers, split into zones: stage, canopy, aisle arches, dining, perimeter.
No dark patches: the cross-aisle, stage steps, lawn edges and the path from parking need low path lights; mark step edges with LED strip.
Power: silent generator plus standby, separate circuits per zone, RCCB/ELCB on every outdoor run, IP65 fixtures and connectors (night dew), cables under mats.
Rain plan: the fabric swags aren't waterproof; keep a clear-roof option or a covered fallback, and check the forecast a week out.
Insects: lights draw bugs — put bug-zappers away from dining and the stage, use citronella along the palm row.
Clubhouse spill: ask the club to switch off or warm up the facade lights so they don't overpower the décor.

How these were measured
Footprints are read off Esri World Imagery at 0.585 m/pixel with a 5 m grid laid over it. Three independent checks agree: the lawn fits the club's own 40 × 20 m handball pitch (KH = Kodanchery Handball Club); the 3 × 2 m handball goal in photo 2 puts the camera 5.3 m away from both its width and height; and the ~45-car ring in the drone shot fits a ≈30 m circle on the red ground. Heights come from floor counts (~3.5 m per floor).
Planning implications
The stage sits behind the lawn, so the whole lawn in front of it is usable: about 20 × 42 m (~840 m²). With a centre aisle that seats roughly 600–700 theatre-style, or 420–520 at banquet rounds. For 700–1,500 guests, the red-earth ground beside it (~1,700 m², level, directly connected) is the natural overflow for dining or parking.
